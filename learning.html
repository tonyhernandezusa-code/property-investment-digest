Learning Center

Plain-language explanations of common measurements used to analyze direct real-estate investments.

NOI

Net operating income is effective property income minus operating expenses, before loan payments and income taxes.

Cap Rate

NOI divided by property price. It compares the property’s unleveraged operating income with its price.

DSCR

Debt-service coverage ratio is NOI divided by annual debt service.

Cash-on-Cash Return

Annual pre-tax cash flow divided by the cash invested.

Break-even Occupancy

The occupancy level generally needed for income to cover operating expenses and debt service.

Scenario Analysis

Changing rent, vacancy, expenses, interest rates, or reserves shows sensitivity to different conditions.

Debt Yield

NOI divided by the loan amount. It measures the property's income relative to the lender's exposure and does not depend on the interest rate or amortization schedule.

Gross Rent Multiplier

Purchase price divided by annual gross scheduled rent. It is a quick comparison measure that does not account for expenses, vacancy, or financing.

Equity Multiple

Total projected cash distributions divided by the initial cash invested. It does not show how quickly the distributions occur.

IRR

Internal rate of return is a time-sensitive estimate based on projected cash flows and a future sale assumption. It is highly dependent on the assumptions entered.

Scheduled vs. Occupied Rent

Scheduled rent assumes every listed unit pays its stated rent. Occupied rent includes only unit rows marked occupied. An additional vacancy allowance may be applied for collection loss or turnover.

Property Equity

Current estimated property value minus the current loan balance. Both inputs must be independently verified, and an estimated value is not an appraisal.

Local Browser Storage

The Property Manager saves data on the current device and browser. It is not a cloud account. Exporting regular backup files protects against browser-data loss.

Weighted County Average

A state comparison formed by combining county values and giving larger counties more weight. It is not the same as an official statewide median.

Permits per 1,000 Housing Units

New housing units authorized relative to the existing housing stock. This makes differently sized states easier to compare.

Home-Value-to-Income Ratio

The weighted county home-value figure divided by weighted county household income. It gives broad context but does not determine affordability for a particular household.

ACS 5-Year Estimate

A survey estimate combining five years of responses. It provides broad geographic coverage but is not a live monthly market measurement.

Non-Overlapping Comparison

Comparing 2020–2024 with 2015–2019 avoids reusing the same survey years in both periods.

Source Permission Register

A record showing which sources are active, planned, free, paid, or awaiting written permission.

Unemployment Rate

The number of unemployed people as a percentage of the labor force. It does not measure everyone who is not working.

Percentage Points

A change from 4.0% to 4.5% is an increase of 0.5 percentage points, not a 0.5% increase.

House Price Index

An index tracks average price movement over time. It is not a median sale price and does not estimate the value of a particular home.

Fair Market Rent

A HUD gross-rent benchmark used in federal housing programs. It is not necessarily the rent currently advertised for a particular unit.

Gross Rent

Shelter rent plus applicable tenant-paid utilities. HUD FMR excludes telephone, cable or satellite television, and Internet service.

Federal Disaster Declaration

An official federal declaration connected to a specific incident. It does not by itself measure the risk or insurability of an individual property.

ADR — Average Daily Rate

Room revenue divided by occupied room nights.

RevPAR

Revenue per available room. It can be calculated as room revenue divided by available room nights or approximately ADR multiplied by occupancy.

TRevPAR

Total hotel revenue divided by available room nights, including room and non-room revenue.

GOPPAR

Gross operating profit per available room. The exact accounting definition depends on the operating statement; this site's version is explicitly described in the methodology.

Price per Key

Hotel purchase price divided by the number of rooms or keys.

ARM / Adjustable-Rate Loan

A loan whose interest rate can change according to its index, margin, reset schedule, floor and cap. Commercial real-estate loans may also be described as floating-rate loans.

Index + Margin

The index is the reference rate named in the loan documents, such as SOFR, Prime or a Treasury benchmark. The margin or spread is added to the index to determine the contractual rate, subject to the loan's other terms.

Rate Floor and Cap

A floor can keep the rate from falling below a stated level. A cap can limit the rate, but actual loan documents may contain several different caps and adjustment rules.

Buyer-Broker Compensation

The compensation agreed between a buyer and the buyer's real-estate broker. Compensation is negotiable. Depending on the transaction, some or all may be paid by the seller or another party, or may remain a buyer expense.

Seller Credit / Concession

An amount the seller agrees to contribute toward eligible buyer transaction costs. Loan-program, appraisal, loan-to-value, and underwriting rules can limit how credits are used.

Cash to Close

In this calculator, estimated cash to close equals the down payment plus net other closing costs plus buyer-paid brokerage. The calculator does not automatically add buyer-broker compensation to the mortgage balance.

Principal & Interest

The mortgage payment attributable to repaying principal and paying interest; taxes, insurance, association dues, PMI and other costs are separate.

HOA / Condo Dues

Recurring association charges. Condo and townhouse buyers should also review reserves, insurance responsibilities, pending projects and possible special assessments.

Special Assessment

An association charge outside ordinary recurring dues. It may be monthly, one-time, or structured differently.

Home Affordability Ratios

Front-end and back-end ratios compare housing and total debt obligations with gross income. The calculator treats them as user-entered assumptions, not universal lender standards.

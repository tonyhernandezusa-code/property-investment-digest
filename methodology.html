State Market Research methodology

State totals are aggregated from the county records used by the County Investment Map. Growth rates compare aggregated current and comparison-period totals. Dollar figures are weighted averages of county medians, and unemployment is population-weighted. The ranking table orders one selected measurement at a time and does not create an overall “best market” score.

Census ACS automated calculations

Migration 5 compares 2020–2024 ACS 5-Year estimates with 2015–2019 ACS 5-Year estimates. Vacancy rate equals vacant units divided by housing units. Owner and renter shares use occupied tenure counts. Home-value-to-income and annual-rent-to-income are transparent ratios based on reported ACS medians.

BLS and FHFA calculations

The unemployment-rate change is the latest seasonally adjusted state rate minus the rate for the same month one year earlier, expressed in percentage points. FHFA quarter, one-year, and five-year changes are calculated from the seasonally adjusted purchase-only index. The since-1991 figure compares the latest index with the 1991 base of 100.

HUD and FEMA calculations

State HUD summaries use the median, minimum, and maximum of the listed county/town FMR records within each state. They are not population-weighted statewide FMR estimates. The annual two-bedroom-FMR-to-income ratio divides twelve times the median listed two-bedroom FMR by ACS median household income.

FEMA summaries count each state/disaster-number combination once because the source contains separate rows for declared counties and other geographic areas. The rolling window begins January 1 of the year ten years before the update year.

Hotel analysis methodology

The Hotel Investment Analysis is a transparent educational model using user-entered occupancy, ADR, revenue, expense and financing assumptions. RevPAR equals room revenue divided by available room nights. TRevPAR equals total revenue divided by available room nights. The model's simplified GOP subtracts variable expenses, fixed/semi-fixed operating expenses, franchise fees and management fees from total revenue. NOI then subtracts property taxes, insurance, the FF&E reserve and other fixed charges before debt service and income taxes.

The pro forma compounds the user's ADR, other-revenue and expense growth assumptions and changes occupancy by the stated annual percentage-point assumption. The illustrative exit value uses Year 11 NOI divided by the user-entered exit cap rate. It is not an appraisal or forecast.

Hotel market methodology

Census County Business Patterns records are filtered to NAICS 721110, legal form 001 (all establishments) and employment-size class 001 (all establishments). Current 2023 results are compared with 2019. Growth percentages are calculated from the published aggregate counts.

Balloon loan methodology

Where a loan term is shorter than the amortization period, the remaining principal balance is shown as a balloon payment due at maturity. In the Hotel Analysis, an interest-only period keeps principal unchanged until amortizing payments begin. If the balloon occurs during the 10-year hotel projection, the model treats it as an additional equity cash requirement and assumes no refinance. Users should model an actual refinance separately because future rates, proceeds, fees, and underwriting are unknown.

Adjustable / floating-rate loan methodology

The Full Property Calculator and Hotel Analysis can model a fixed-rate loan or an adjustable/floating-rate stress scenario. During the user-entered initial fixed period, the initial interest rate is used. At the first reset, the modeled rate equals the user-entered reference-index assumption plus the user-entered margin or spread, subject to the entered floor and lifetime cap. At later reset dates, the reference-index assumption changes by the user-entered annual percentage-point stress assumption. Payments are recalculated at each modeled rate change using the then-current principal balance and remaining amortization period.

This model does not retrieve or forecast future SOFR, Prime, Treasury, or other benchmark rates. Actual loan documents may use lookback periods, day-count conventions, periodic caps, minimum rate changes, replacement-index provisions, default-rate provisions, hedges, swaps, or other features not represented here. Users should enter the contractual terms that are relevant to their own analysis and verify results against lender documents.

Buyer brokerage and cash-to-close methodology

The Full Property Calculator permits an optional buyer-broker compensation amount entered either as a percentage of purchase price or a flat dollar amount. Any seller/listing-side payment toward that compensation reduces the buyer-paid portion, but never below zero. A separate seller concession or closing credit reduces the calculator's other estimated closing costs, but never below zero.

Estimated cash to close / cash invested equals down payment + net other closing costs + buyer-paid brokerage. The loan amount continues to be calculated from purchase price and down-payment percentage; the model does not automatically increase the loan balance to finance buyer-broker compensation or closing costs. Actual lender treatment, seller-concession limits, eligible costs, appraisal requirements, and loan-to-value calculations vary by loan program and transaction and should be verified with the lender and settlement professionals.

Home & condo mortgage methodology

The residential calculator determines mortgage principal from purchase price less the entered down payment. Fixed payments use the entered rate and term. ARM stress scenarios hold the initial rate through the entered fixed period, then use the entered index assumption plus margin subject to floor and cap, recalculating the payment when the modeled rate changes.

Cash to close is modeled as down payment + net closing costs + buyer-paid brokerage + inspection + one-time special assessment + other entered one-time costs. Buyer brokerage and closing costs are not automatically added to the mortgage balance.

Home affordability methodology

The affordability page solves two limits: an income/debt limit based on the entered front-end and back-end ratios, and a cash limit based on available cash, down payment, net closing costs, buyer-paid brokerage and fixed buyer costs. The displayed illustrative price is the lower of the two. Maintenance is shown for budgeting but excluded from the modeled DTI.

Authorized Property Search security methodology

The browser page uses Firebase email/password authentication and sends the signed-in user’s short-lived Firebase ID token to the Cloudflare Worker in the Authorization header. The Worker must independently validate that token and compare the authenticated email with a server-side allow-list before returning protected property data. Hiding the page or checking a password only in browser JavaScript is not considered sufficient protection.

The ATTOM API credential remains server-side in the Cloudflare Worker. The public GitHub Pages files must never contain the ATTOM secret.

SEC stock-fundamentals methodology

The stock updater retrieves issuer submissions and XBRL company facts from SEC EDGAR. Annual income-statement and cash-flow measurements use standardized fiscal-year facts from 10-K/10-K/A filings when available. Balance-sheet measurements use the latest standardized 10-Q or 10-K fact available for the selected tag.

Revenue growth, net-income growth and diluted-EPS growth compare the latest identified annual value with the preceding annual value. Net margin equals annual net income divided by annual revenue. Modeled free cash flow equals operating cash flow minus capital expenditures when both are available. Liabilities-to-assets and equity-to-assets use the latest identified balance-sheet values.

Companies can use different XBRL tags and reporting definitions, so missing or non-comparable values are displayed as unavailable rather than estimated. No stock-price prediction, buy/sell score or price target is calculated.

Authorized Yahoo stock-development methodology

The development stock updater retrieves daily historical prices through yfinance. One-day and six-month changes are calculated from adjusted daily closing prices. The 14-day RSI uses rolling average gains and losses over daily closes. The 52-week range uses the downloaded daily high and low series. Market capitalization, trailing P/E and dividend yield are displayed only when returned by yfinance.

Major indexes, index futures and commodities use the same daily-history mechanism. The complete output is encrypted with AES-256-GCM using a key derived by PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256 from a password stored only as a GitHub Actions repository secret. The public webpage decrypts the payload locally only after the user enters that password.
